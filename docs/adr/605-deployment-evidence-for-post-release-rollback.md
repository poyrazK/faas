# ADR-605: Deployment evidence for post-release automatic rollback

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-05
- **Context:** ADR-604 pins completed-release recovery to an exact predecessor, but the alert evaluator uses app-wide metric windows. An older deployment's failures can remain in that window after a healthy replacement finishes. The fire timestamp establishes eligibility, not attribution of the failures.
- **Decision:** APID's existing historical rollback acceptance transaction requires exact candidate request telemetry before creating the checked rollback operation or claiming the release. Only `error_rate_pct` with `gt` or `gte` qualifies initially. Count publisher-weighted 2xx and 5xx responses, matching the alert denominator, with at least 20 requests and at least one server error. Unsupported metrics fail closed; active rollout recovery retains its existing semantics. No meterd deployment writer is added.
- **Window:** Capture the candidate completion timestamp and the rule metric, comparison, threshold and window with the production fire. Fix the observation end to `floor_minute(fired_at - 30 seconds)` and the start to the later of the configured window start or the first complete minute after cutover. Exclude the entire partial cutover minute and unfinished ingestion minutes. Reads constrain account, app, and exact deployment ID. A qualifying sample must be within two minutes of the observation end. Retries may ingest delayed rows into this same fixed window for at most two minutes after the fire; they cannot accumulate later requests. Very early fires or low-volume apps can lack sufficient evidence.
- **Atomicity and recovery:** Compare the pinned rule settings, cutover and exact pair against fresh facts under the app/rule locks, then read telemetry and save the qualification, intent audit, release claim and checked operation in one transaction. Recheck cutover and deadlines after acquiring deployment locks; failure rolls back the claim, target preparation and queued notification. Healthy evidence terminates that fire without preparing a predecessor. Insufficient, stale, or unavailable evidence blocks with an explicit code until the deadline; deadline expiry terminates the fire. Accepted evidence is immutable and survives rule changes, restarts, later ingestion and accepted cleanup. Previously accepted operations continue; unaccepted legacy receipts without a pinned snapshot cannot acquire intent. Binding checks, artifact verification, readiness, routing, service ACK/drain and prevention of rollback chains remain required.
- **Memory and compatibility:** ADR-127 production telemetry is PostgreSQL-only. MemStore exposes unavailable evidence and never substitutes the app-wide observed value. Receipt JSON gains an optional `deployment_evidence` object; no schema migration is needed. CLI human/JSON receipts show the observation window, counts, comparison and reason. GET and wait remain read-only, and wait rejects changes to pinned evidence or to accepted counts. OpenAPI and Go, Node and Python SDKs carry the evidence.
- **Validation:** Deterministic window, deadline and comparison boundaries; PostgreSQL account/app/deployment isolation, old-release errors, cutover buckets, unfinished minutes, weighted counts, 4xx denominator exclusion, healthy replacements, low traffic, delayed ingestion, staleness, changed threshold/window/cutover, expiry while waiting for a target lock and immutable accepted evidence. Function/service recovery tests retain exact intent, binding fences and service completion barriers; APID restarts resume the same qualified operation. CLI wait rejects changed evidence and SDKs round-trip it.
- **Limits:** This is observed request evidence, not a complete accounting of all production requests. Missing telemetry, unsupported metrics, or low traffic cannot authorize automatic historical rollback. Manual checked rollback remains available.
