## Context

Gregale's tiers, from most expensive to hold to cheapest: running, quiet
pause, frozen, parked (snapshot restore), cold boot. The product goal is to
keep most apps, services and functions in the upper tiers without adding
latency, while the customer pays only for work and the platform holds as
little RAM as possible. The case to be good at is an app that sits idle for
an hour.

### What production does today (rc.326, measured 2026-11-07)

| Tier | Wake (added to the first request) | RAM held | Admission books | Billed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Running, idle | 0 | everything the guest touched | measured + plan reservation | yes |
| Quiet pause (vmmd, 2 s after the last request; functions and `between_requests` only) | ~1 ms (mean 1.1, p90 2.4) | same | same as running | yes (ledger and meterd switches are off) |
| Frozen (schedd, at the idle timeout) | thaw 61–129 ms in schedd: vmmd 10–18 ms, publish 30–85 ms | same: an idle freeze does not reclaim | `max(resident, 8 MB) × 1.25` | no |
| Parked | snapshot restore, 0.5–0.65 s at the gateway | 0 | 0 | ~0.9 s of WAKING per wake |
| Cold boot | 1.1–6 s | 0 | 0 | the boot |

An app idle for an hour on Scale's defaults runs billed for its 600 s idle
timeout, sits frozen at full RAM for 600 s more, then parks. Its next request
pays a restore.

### Measurements

All on production-us between 08:16 and 12:35 UTC, with the owner's test apps
and throwaway test apps on the Scale bot account. Scripts and raw data are
listed under "Evidence".

1. **Billing.** Driving one function (h10-fn-go, 1 GB) and one app
   (h10-hello-node, 256 MB) through four traffic patterns:
   - unbilled quiet pause would cut billed seconds by 79% at one request
     every 10 s, 82% with a burst every 20 s, 88% at one request every
     2 min, and ~3.5% at a steady 1 req/s;
   - freeze/thaw changes the bill by 0.4–4.8%. It stops billing at the same
     moment a park would and only drops the restore's billed WAKING
     (mean 0.90 s) and a park's SNAPSHOTTING (mean 0.25 s);
   - meterd's `usage_minutes` matched `instance_billing_intervals` to the
     second in all eight windows;
   - fleet, last 24 h: 81.1 GB-h billed, of which about 60 GB-h (≈74%) is
     apps waiting out their idle timeout (806 idle-outs, mean ≈554 s). Apps
     default to `when_idle`, so neither freeze nor unbilled quiet pause
     touches that.
2. **What a frozen VM holds.** All of its touched memory: a frozen VM keeps
   18–144 MB on small apps and 620–700 MB on a 600 MB heap. Proactive
   compression (ADR-20261031143521) only starts above 85% of a node's
   ceiling and compresses the whole VM; it ran 0 times in the last 24 h.
3. **Full compression** (zswap, lz4, memory-only, on fsn-2 and fsn-3):
   - about 3.9× (a 600 MB heap: 620 → 167 MB);
   - compressing costs ~1.5–2 ms of one host core per MB, off the request
     path;
   - a light request after the thaw costs +20–75 ms;
   - every compressed MB the guest touches later costs ~5.5–6 ms once
     (~21–25 µs per 4 KB page; item 5 splits this into decompression and
     second-level faults): +170 ms for a request that touches a 30 MB
     working set, 3.4–3.5 s to read 600 MB.

   Full compression can therefore wake slower than a snapshot restore.
4. **Cold-page compression.** On a 630 MB guest (600 MB written once,
   30 MB working set touched by every request): an MGLRU aging pass on the
   VM's memcg, then `memory.reclaim` coldest first.

   | Compressed | Frozen VM | First working-set request |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0% | 659–698 MB | 30–32 ms |
   | 75% | 301–302 MB | 32–33 ms |
   | 90% | 177–183 MB | 200–206 ms |

   At 90% the reclaim reached the working set. An aging pass costs about
   0.9 ms per MB touched next.
5. **Bulk prefetch.** The same 630 MB guest, fully compressed while frozen
   (670 → 181–187 MB). Before the thaw, the swapped pages were read back from
   the host through `/proc/PID/pagemap` and `/proc/PID/mem`, with 1 or 4
   threads:

   | Before the thaw | Read back 658 MB | First working-set request (30 MB) | Reading all 630 MB |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | not compressed (control) | — | 1.7–1.9 ms | 27–29 ms |
   | compressed, no prefetch | — | 168–181 ms | 3.36–3.50 s |
   | prefetch, 1 thread | 525–607 ms (1.1–1.3 GB/s) | 132–138 ms | 2.65–2.85 s |
   | prefetch, 4 threads | 227–332 ms (2.0–2.9 GB/s) | 132–140 ms | 2.69–2.76 s |

   - Bulk decompression on the host costs 0.35–0.9 ms per MB, against
     ~5.5 ms per MB (≈21 µs per page) when the guest faults pages back one
     at a time.
   - Prefetch removes only ~25% of what the guest pays. The other ~4.3 ms per
     MB (≈17 µs per page) is the guest's second-level (EPT) faults. Reclaim
     dropped those mappings, and every first touch still faults even when the
     page is back in host RAM.
   - On nested GCE, each of those faults exits to the outer hypervisor.
     Decompression is therefore not the dominant cost on these hosts.
6. **Caveat.** production-us compute nodes are GCE VMs, so tenant VMs run on
   nested KVM. Per-fault costs (items 3–5) are likely lower on the
   bare-metal reference node, and the constants below must be re-measured
   there before they are tuned.

### Constraints from the owner

- No level may add more than 100 ms to the first request.
- No level may wake slower than the level below it: a freeze must never wake
  slower than a snapshot restore.
- No control-plane Postgres on the request path.
- Billing stays plan RAM + 8 MB per billed second (§4.7).

## Decision

### 1. The ladder

Timers count from the end of the last request. Any request at any level
returns the instance to running.

| Level | Default entry | Where | Wake | RAM | Billed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 running | — | — | 0 | all | yes |
| L1 quiet pause | 1 s quiet | vmmd | ~1 ms | all | no |
| L2 freeze | 10 s quiet | vmmd | ~1 ms | all; admission books measured | no |
| L3 freeze, cold-compressed | memory untouched ≥ 1 min is compressed | vmmd | resume + faults on cold pages only | shrinks | no |
| L4 freeze, deeper | memory untouched ≥ 3 min is compressed, within the app's wake budget | vmmd | same | shrinks further | no |
| L5 snapshot | 10 min quiet, or earlier under node memory pressure, largest and longest idle first | schedd park | snapshot restore | 0 | no |

- These timers are defaults; §4 makes them per app.
- `api.QuietPauseAfterMillis` goes from 2000 to 1000.
- New limits (`IdleFreezeAfter`, `IdleColdAge1`, `IdleColdAge2`,
  `IdleSnapshotAfter`, `IdleWakeBudget`) go in `pkg/api/limits.go`.

### 2. A wake never costs more than the level below

Each level's wake budget is `min(100 ms, the app's measured restore time)` of
added first-request time.

- **How vmmd measures:** it times every wake from L3/L4 as the resume plus the
  fault time spent on compressed pages before the guest goes idle again
  (`vmmd_idle_wake_seconds{level}`).
- **Compression ceiling:** each app has one, as a page age. When a wake
  exceeds the budget, the ceiling moves to older pages only; when wakes stay
  under half the budget, it may move younger.
- **Full compression:** never used on a VM that may still wake. It stays a
  pressure step that runs immediately before a park.
- **Percentages:** compression is by age, never by percentage (90% reached
  the working set in the measurement).

### 3. L1–L4 live in vmmd; schedd keeps the instance state machine

L1–L4 are quiet pause extended: vmmd pauses the VM in place and then compresses
its cold pages by age.

- **Instance row:** stays RUNNING. There is no schedd thaw and no
  control-plane write per request, so the 30–85 ms publish step leaves these
  wakes.
- **State machine:** schedd remains the only writer to `instances` and owns
  the move to PARKED (L5).
- **Telemetry:** vmmd reports each instance's level, resident bytes and
  compressed bytes in `InstanceStats` / `InstanceTelemetry` (additive,
  `omitempty`).
- **Admission:** schedd books an instance at L2+ as frozen instances are
  booked today (`FrozenChargeMB`, measured with an 8 MB floor), leaves it out
  of the plan-reservation sum, and does not count its vCPU. The vCPU part
  already holds: L2+ instances are quiet-paused, and quiet pause admission
  discounts them. The floor ships with L1/L2 (#6739). Leaving L2+ out of the
  reservation sum needs the durable per-node guard
  (`pkg/state/node_reservation.go`) to take a RAM discount, the way it takes
  the quiet pause CPU discount. The guard sums RUNNING rows, and it must
  agree with the ledger, or wakes routed to a node the ledger sees room on
  are refused there. The ledger and the guard take the same discount, at
  most `api.IdleLadderReservationDiscountMaxPercent` (200) of the ceiling
  (#6746).
- **Schedd FROZEN state:** stays only while existing freezes drain. New idle
  periods use the vmmd levels when the `idle_ladder` switch is on.
- **Idle-out:** with schedd's `idle_ladder_idle_out` switch on, the reaper
  leaves an instance vmmd reports at level 1 or deeper RUNNING past its idle
  timeout and parks it at L5 instead of freezing it. A longer configured
  idle timeout still applies. Measured-memory eviction takes such an
  instance with the frozen ones, before any instance inside its idle
  timeout. The switch needs the pause ledger, and it holds only the
  accounts that the `quiet_pause_unbilled` platform flag releases, the same
  decision meterd makes. Every other account keeps its idle timeout, so
  nobody is billed for the longer residency.

### 4. Per-app timing

- **Cron:** schedd files an ADR-160 prewarm intent for an app parked at L5
  before its next cron run, so the run lands on L0 instead of waiting on a
  restore. The prewarm drain already enforces plan limits, placement and
  RAM. (Built as Amendment 3.)
- **Adaptive timers (second step):** schedd keeps each app's recent
  inter-arrival times (`request_telemetry`). An app whose next request
  usually comes within the L5 timer stays at L3/L4 while the node has room;
  an app that goes quiet for hours goes down the ladder sooner.
- **Bounds:** the defaults in §1 stay the upper bound, and node pressure
  always wins.

### 4a. How L3 and L4 are built

vmmd's `idle_ladder_compress` switch builds L3 and L4 on the kernel's
multi-gen LRU (`/sys/kernel/debug/lru_gen`). The switch needs
`idle_ladder = "on"`.

- **Aging:** an aging pass opens a new youngest generation and moves the
  pages accessed since the previous pass into it. vmmd ages a quiet-paused
  VM's cgroup only after the guest ran, and at most once per
  `IdleLadderColdAge1Seconds`. Each generation therefore spans at least a
  minute of the guest's activity, and paused time never ages the working
  set.
- **Page age in practice:** "untouched for that long" means "in an older
  generation than the youngest few".
  - L3 compresses every generation but the youngest 3
    (`IdleLadderCold1KeepGenerations`).
  - L4 compresses every generation but the youngest 2
    (`IdleLadderCold2KeepGenerations`). The kernel never evicts fewer, and
    keeps at most 4.
- **Fresh generations only:** a pause compresses only after it has aged once
  itself. Otherwise the pages that the last wake brought back could sit in
  stale old generations and be compressed again.
- **Swap:** the scope's swap opens only for the compression, into
  memory-only zswap, and closes after the next resume.
- **Wake cost:** a wake is charged the resume plus
  `IdleLadderFaultMicrosPerPage` (21 µs, nested) for every page the cgroup
  brings back (`zswpin`, `pswpin`, `workingset_refault_file`) before the
  next pause. This is an estimate of §2's fault time. The constant must be
  re-measured on bare metal (Rollout step 1).
- **Budget:** `IdleLadderWakeBudgetMillis`, or the instance's own restore
  time when that is shorter.
- **Ceiling:** an app whose wake exceeds the budget keeps one more
  generation at each level, up to `IdleLadderMaxExtraKeepGenerations`. Five
  wakes in a row under half the budget give one back.
- **Never blocks a request:** compression works in 16 MB chunks without the
  pause lock and stops at the first chunk after a resume.
- **Cost at L5:** parking snapshots the guest's memory, so a park from L3/L4
  first brings the compressed pages back. That is a one-off cost off the
  request path. Measure it on bare metal before the switch is on widely.

### 5. Prefetch on wake

On a wake from L3 or L4, vmmd starts bringing the VM's compressed pages back
in the background, most recently used first. It runs alongside the resumed
guest, so pages are back before the guest asks for them.

On the nested production hosts this saves only ~25% (Context, item 5),
because the guest's second-level faults remain. So:

- **Budget:** the §2 wake budget is computed without prefetch.
- **Prefetch as an option:** prefetch ships behind its own switch, and only
  if the bare-metal measurement (Rollout step 1) shows the faults left after
  a prefetch are cheap there.
- **The larger win:** pre-populating the guest's second-level page tables
  after the prefetch would remove the remaining cost. `KVM_PRE_FAULT_MEMORY`
  (Linux 6.10+) does this, but it is a vCPU ioctl that Firecracker owns, so
  it needs a Firecracker change. It is measured before anyone builds it.

### 6. Pausing is safe by default

vmmd pauses a request-mode instance only when it has no request or stream in
flight and its cgroup CPU and tap traffic show it is idle. (The traffic is
read on the namespace's root-side veth; see Amendment 2.)

- **Apps with background work:** an app whose timers, heartbeats or
  background threads keep it busy stays at L0 and stays billed, because it is
  working. This guard is what makes pausing a safe default for apps, not only
  for functions and `between_requests`.
- **Opt-out:** `lifecycle.pause: never` remains.
- **Never paused:** workers, services, jobs, app tasks, mirrors and
  private-network apps, as today.
- **Defaults:** new request-mode apps default to the guarded pause. Existing
  apps keep `when_idle` until they opt in (a customer-contract change, owner
  decision).

### 7. Billing

A billed second is an L0 second: plan RAM + 8 MB while the instance is
running and not paused.

- **L1–L4:** unbilled through ADR-20261023171905's ledger (vmmd reports,
  schedd writes `instance_quiet_pauses`, meterd subtracts what the ledger
  proves). Its failure rule stays: anything unproven is billed.
- **L5 and cold boot:** unchanged.
- **Capacity floors:** an app with `--min` still pays for its floor
  (ADR-060).

## Consequences

- **Customers:** billed for active time plus ~1 s per quiet period. Measured
  −79% to −88% for traffic with gaps, and the same for apps once the guarded
  pause is their default.
- **First request:**
  - L1 and L2: ~1 ms;
  - L3 and L4: within the 100 ms budget. Measured +2 ms on a 30 MB working
    set at 75% compression, plus ~25 ms for the aging pass on nested hosts;
  - L5: a restore, as today.
- **RAM:** a frozen VM shrinks with time. A 600 MB heap goes from ~680 MB to
  ~300 MB by L4, a small app from ~26 MB to ~10 MB, and L5 holds nothing.
- **Control plane:** fewer state changes than today's freeze and thaw, since
  L2–L4 are local. Pause ledger writes are batched per node every 2 s.
- **CPU:**
  - compression costs ~2 ms of a host core per MB, only for VMs quiet for
    at least a minute;
  - decompression on touch is charged to the VM's own vCPU time;
  - aging passes are per memcg and cheap to run, but add ~0.9 ms per MB on
    the next touch on nested hosts.
- **Invariant 2 (§6.2):** keeps its 47,600 MB ceiling. Under measured
  admission an L2+ instance counts at its measured charge, as frozen
  instances already do; the spec text must say so.
- **zswap on every compute node:** fsn-3 is a canary; fsn-2 was set up by
  hand on 2026-11-07 (PR #6721 lists it). A node without zswap runs L3/L4 as
  L2.
- **A vmmd restart:** compressed pages stay in zswap and fault back in on
  touch. The level resets to L2 for the new vmmd.

## Alternatives considered

- **Freeze in schedd at 10 s.** Every request after a 10 s gap would become
  a 60–130 ms thaw with control-plane writes, instead of a ~1 ms resume.
- **Full compression at freeze.** Reading a 600 MB heap back costs 3.4 s,
  slower than a restore, which breaks the monotonic rule.
- **Compression by a fixed percentage.** 90% reached the working set
  (+170 ms).
- **Early freeze (ADR-20261021215353).** It puts the control plane on every
  request; superseded by §3.
- **A fixed 10 min to snapshot for every app.** Kept as the default and upper
  bound; §4 shortens or extends it per app within node pressure.

## Amendment 1 (2026-11-17): L3/L4 under kernel lockdown

**Context.** ADR-20261107220000 raised kernel lockdown to `integrity` on
every host the night before L3/L4 merged. Lockdown refuses debugfs to root,
so on production compute nodes the multi-gen LRU's `/sys/kernel/debug/lru_gen`
lists but does not open. Turned on on fsn-2 on 2026-11-17, every aging pass
failed with `operation not permitted` (dmesg: "Lockdown: vmmd: debugfs access
is restricted") and nothing was compressed. The KVM test host has no lockdown,
so the metal run did not see it. Lockdown cannot be lowered while a host runs
and is the security baseline, so L3/L4 work without debugfs.

**Decision.**

1. vmmd probes once whether `lru_gen` opens. If it does, L3/L4 stay as in
   §4a. If it does not, aging is left to the kernel and L3/L4 compress
   through the VM cgroup's `memory.reclaim`, as the frozen tier already does
   (ADR-906): L3 reclaims until `api.IdleLadderCold1KeepPercent` (50%) of the
   VM's memory stays resident, L4 until `api.IdleLadderCold2KeepPercent`
   (25%). The VM's memory is what is resident plus what earlier passes
   compressed (`memory.stat` `zswapped`), so L4 keeps its share of the same
   whole as L3. The kernel reclaims the coldest pages first and checks the
   pages' access bits as it does. The 2026-11-07 measurement on a 630 MB guest
   compressed 75% at +2 ms on the next request and reached the working set at
   90% (+170 ms).
2. The per-app ceiling works in the same steps: each wake over budget keeps
   `api.IdleLadderExtraKeepPercent` (25%) more at each level, and a level that
   would keep 100% compresses nothing (outcome `ceiling`). Wake cost is
   measured as before, from `memory.stat` refaults, which lockdown leaves open.
3. Measured admission charges an instance vmmd reports at L3 or L4
   (`api.IdleLadderColdLevel`) its latest resident sample, as it charges a
   frozen instance (ADR 20261031190030). Charging the 10-minute window's p95,
   as for L2, would hold the pre-compression size until about the 10-minute
   mark, where the ladder parks the instance, so compression would never reach
   admission. What stays resident after a compression is the share its wakes
   showed it needs; eviction above the ceiling still bounds a node if many
   compressed instances resume at once. On resume the window's p95 applies
   again.
4. Metal and node runs of L3/L4 must run with lockdown `integrity`, the
   production setting.

**Consequences.** L3/L4 work on every host. Without explicit aging the kernel
chooses the pages by its own view of recency, so the first wakes of an app may
refault more than with generations; the ceiling adapts within a few wakes.

## Amendment 2 (2026-11-19): the guard's inbound signal and br-tenants flooding

**Context.** With `on` + `apps` on both production nodes, guarded apps paused
about 300 times an hour per instance and stayed paused about 93% of the time.
About 78% of the resumes on fsn-2 were `inbound`: the guard saw the root-side
veth's tx bytes move. A 10-minute capture on one paused app's veth on fsn-2
(e2edp-1115-r) saw 35 frames, all flooded by br-tenants, and no unicast:
18 IPv6 router solicitations, 12 MLD reports, 3 duplicate address checks and
2 ARP broadcasts. Every namespace that comes up sends these, and the bridge
floods them to every port. 21 of the app's 31 pauses ended within 0.8 s of
one of those frames (median pause 1.0 s); the other 10 ended with a request
(median 53 s). None of the frames reaches a guest: the namespace routes to
tap0 and does not forward link-local traffic. The cost is small today, but it
grows with paused apps × namespaces started per node, and it made the
`inbound` metric measure neighbours starting up.

**Decision.** A tenant's br-tenants port no longer takes other ports'
broadcast and multicast (`netns.TenantPortFloodOffCommand`: `bridge_slave
mcast_flood off bcast_flood off`, set at namespace setup and on adoption).

- The kernel still floods what the bridge device itself sends, such as the
  host's ARP for a lease, and unknown unicast still floods.
- Tenants reach each other only through the host, so nothing used
  tenant-to-tenant L2 flooding. Private networks have their own bridges and
  keep it.
- The guard keeps reading the root-side veth, not tap0 as §6 says. Inside
  the namespace, tap0 also carries the namespace's own traffic to the guest:
  the request forwarder's TCP keepalives every 15 s on idle connections, and
  ARP reachability probes a few seconds after a request. Neither crosses the
  veth. With flooding off, the veth carries only traffic from outside the
  namespace, which is what the guard means by inbound.

**Consequences.** Paused guarded apps resume on requests and on real inbound
traffic, not when other apps start. The veth ingress meter (ADR-048,
informational) stops counting other tenants' noise. Tenants stop seeing each
other's MAC and link-local addresses. A port made before this change gets the
flags when vmmd adopts its guest at the next restart.

## Amendment 3 (2026-11-19): cron pre-wake

**Context.** §4's first step, built. In the 24 hours to 2026-11-19 16:30Z
production-us fired 249 HTTP cron runs on 10 apps:

- 189 found their app frozen and thawed it (p50 45 ms).
- 12 found it running.
- 47 found it parked and restored a snapshot (p50 0.79 s, p90 1.0 s, max
  3.1 s), and 1 cold-booted (2.1 s).

The last 48 are what this step removes.

**Decision.**

1. **When.** On each minute's cron tick, after the sweep's first pass,
   schedd looks `api.CronPrewakeLeadSeconds` (60 s) ahead. For each app it
   takes the earliest HTTP cron fire in that window. Command crons run in
   their own job VM and are skipped.
2. **Which apps.** All of these must hold:
   - the app has no resident instance (no row that counts for RAM, frozen
     included);
   - this schedd owns it;
   - the cron passes the sweep's own gates: plan limit, the `crons`
     capability, and the compute budget and spend cap;
   - the app's account is one the `quiet_pause_unbilled` platform flag
     releases.

   An app with a cron due now, or one that fired within the lead, is left
   alone. That fire's own wake is still coming up, and an intent beside it
   could admit a second instance.
3. **How.** schedd files an ADR-160 prewarm intent:
   - trigger `cron` (already allowed by the table's CHECK), count 1;
   - `wake_at` the fire time, `expires_at` `api.CronPrewakeHoldSeconds`
     (120 s) after it.

   The drain's lead window is also 60 s, so it claims the intent at once and
   wakes the app through the normal admission path, which keeps plan,
   placement, RAM and wake-rate limits. The intent's floor keeps the reaper
   from parking the instance before the run. The ladder quiet-pauses it after
   1 s and freezes it in vmmd after 10 s (L2), so the run resumes it in about
   a millisecond. A schedd that files twice (a restart within the minute)
   files a target of 1 twice, which admits nothing more.
4. **Billing.** Only the pause ledger leaves the early seconds unbilled,
   hence the flag gate: it is the same decision meterd and the idle-out make.
   The restore and the first quiet second are billed, as the run's own wake
   would have been.
5. **Retention.** The hourly retention tick deletes settled `cron` intents
   `api.CronPrewakeIntentRetention` (3 days) after their window ended.
   Intents the API filed keep their lifecycle read-back.
6. **Switch.** schedd `cron_prewake` (`FAAS_CRON_PREWAKE`) is off by
   default. It is ignored, with a warning, unless the prewarm drain runs
   (`FAAS_PREWARM_ENABLED=1`, on in the unit since ADR-1189).

**Consequences.** A released app's cron runs find it at L1 or L2 instead of
L5. Each pre-wake moves that app's restore up to a minute ahead of the cron
wave. It holds the app's RAM from then on, booked at L2's discount once it
freezes. Per-app adaptive timers (§4's second step) are still to come.

## Amendment 4 (2026-11-22): resume on packets addressed to the instance

**Context.** On the OVH nodes (rc.371, Amendment 2's flood flags in place),
about 85% of guarded-app resumes still came from no request: in 6 hours
about 5,500 resumes against about 840 request gaps. A 2-minute capture on a
paused app's root-side veth on bhs-2 (frontend) matched 27 of its 28 pause
ends, each within 0.75 s, to a frame meant for someone else. The app
received no requests in that time. The frames were of two kinds:

- The host's ARP broadcasts, such as 24 one-second retries for an address
  whose namespace did not answer. The bridge floods what the bridge device
  itself sends whatever the port flags say.
- Unknown unicast that the bridge floods to every port. A paused namespace
  sends nothing, so its MAC ages out of the bridge after 300 s, and the
  next packet for it goes to every port: a late reply on an old connection,
  or the host connecting to an address with no VM behind it.

Each such frame resumed every paused guarded app on the node, and each
resume bills at least the 1 s quiet window before the next pause. With
quiet pause unbilled for every account (ADR-20261023171905), those resumes
are most of what an idle app still pays.

**Decision.** The guard resumes a paused app when IPv4 packets addressed to
its network namespace arrive, not on any byte at the veth.

- vmmd reads the namespace's `Ip InReceives` from
  `/proc/<firecracker pid>/net/snmp` every 250 ms
  (`fcvm.NetnsIngressReader`). The jailer starts Firecracker inside the
  namespace.
  - The kernel drops ARP, and frames for another MAC (PACKET_OTHERHOST),
    before it counts IPv4 input. A 10-minute check on bhs-2 saw four such
    frames move the veth (an IPv6 router solicitation, a flooded SYN and two
    ARP broadcasts) while the counter stayed put.
  - Packets for the namespace or forwarded to the guest still count: a
    request, a reply on a connection the app opened, a peer's keepalive.
- IPv6 is not counted. The guest network is IPv4 (ADR-009), and the
  namespace's IPv6 receive counter moves on the multicast the bridge carries.
- The pid is found by its `--id` (as the reaper finds it) and pinned to its
  `ns/net` link. A pid whose link changes, or a VM not yet found, has no
  reading. Without a reading less than 2 s old, the guard falls back to the
  veth's ingress bytes, as before. Without either it resumes the app.
- The idle decision is unchanged: it still allows at most 512 bytes each
  way on the veth over the L1 window.
- `vmmd_idle_ladder_app_resumes_total` gains the cause `inbound_veth` for a
  resume through the fallback. `inbound` now means addressed packets, so
  `inbound_veth` near zero shows the new signal is in use.

**Consequences.** Paused guarded apps stay paused through other tenants'
traffic and the host's neighbour discovery. Their resumes, and the quiet
window each one bills, drop toward their real inbound traffic, and more of
them reach L2 and below. vmmd reads one small proc file per guarded VM per
tick. The host-side causes (ARP for absent addresses, connections to
addresses with no VM, unicast to aged-out MACs) are not fixed here. They no
longer wake guests, but they still cost the bridge a copy per port.

## Amendment 5 (2026-11-24): leave out the namespace's own host-unreachable reports

**Context.** After Amendment 4 reached production (rc.378), resumes from
the veth fallback fell to zero and median pauses grew from 1-4 s to 14-28 s.
Resumes were still about 2.3 times the request gaps. A 10-minute capture
inside three paused apps' namespaces on bhs-3 (2026-11-24) matched 6 of the
11 pause ends of cartservice and currencyservice, each 0.14-0.24 s earlier,
to `lo: ICMP host 10.0.0.2 unreachable`. The namespace had sent it to itself:

- The instance bridge closes an idle connection to the guest (after 1 s
  for HTTP/1.1, 30 s for h2c) when the guest may already be paused, so its
  FIN goes unanswered and is retransmitted.
- The paused guest cannot answer the namespace's neighbour probes for
  10.0.0.2, so the entry fails after its reachable time and the probes.
- The kernel reports the dropped packets to their local sender with an ICMP
  host unreachable. It goes over `lo`, which counts in Ip InReceives.

The guest never sees these reports, but each one woke it 13-67 s into a
pause.

**Decision.** The guard's addressed reading is Ip InReceives less Icmp
InDestUnreachs, from the same `/proc/<firecracker pid>/net/snmp` read.

- Every ICMP message the namespace receives passed IPv4 input first, so the
  two move together for such a report. ICMP forwarded to the guest is not
  counted in Icmp: it still wakes the app.
- Destination unreachable messages from elsewhere for the namespace's own
  sockets (the instance bridge, a relay) are left out too. They are for the
  namespace, not the guest.
- A reading whose Icmp line is missing, or whose InDestUnreachs exceeds
  InReceives, is no reading, and the guard falls back as before.
- Nothing else changes: same poll, same fallback, same causes.

**Consequences.** A paused app that sits behind an idle bridge connection
stays paused until traffic for it arrives. The other source in the capture
(a peer's TCP keepalives every 15 s on an app's idle service connections)
is real traffic for the guest and is fixed at its sender, gatewayd-internal's
guest service proxy, not here.
