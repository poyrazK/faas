# ADR-20261107122928: The idle ladder — every idle second costs less, and no level wakes slower than the one below

## Context

Gregale's tiers, from most expensive to hold to cheapest: running, quiet
pause, frozen, parked (snapshot restore), cold boot. The product goal is to
keep most apps, services and functions in the upper tiers without adding
latency, while the customer pays only for work and the platform holds as
little RAM as possible. The case to be good at is an app that sits idle for
an hour.

### What production does today (rc.326, measured 2026-11-07)

| Tier | Wake (added to the first request) | RAM held | Admission books | Billed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Running, idle | 0 | everything the guest touched | measured + plan reservation | yes |
| Quiet pause (vmmd, 2 s after the last request; functions and `between_requests` only) | ~1 ms (mean 1.1, p90 2.4) | same | same as running | yes (ledger and meterd switches are off) |
| Frozen (schedd, at the idle timeout) | thaw 61–129 ms in schedd: vmmd 10–18 ms, publish 30–85 ms | same: an idle freeze does not reclaim | `max(resident, 8 MB) × 1.25` | no |
| Parked | snapshot restore, 0.5–0.65 s at the gateway | 0 | 0 | ~0.9 s of WAKING per wake |
| Cold boot | 1.1–6 s | 0 | 0 | the boot |

An app idle for an hour on Scale's defaults runs billed for its 600 s idle
timeout, sits frozen at full RAM for 600 s more, then parks. Its next request
pays a restore.

### Measurements

All on production-us between 08:16 and 12:35 UTC, with the owner's test apps
and throwaway test apps on the Scale bot account. Scripts and raw data are
listed under "Evidence".

1. **Billing.** Driving one function (h10-fn-go, 1 GB) and one app
   (h10-hello-node, 256 MB) through four traffic patterns:
   - unbilled quiet pause would cut billed seconds by 79% at one request
     every 10 s, 82% with a burst every 20 s, 88% at one request every
     2 min, and ~3.5% at a steady 1 req/s;
   - freeze/thaw changes the bill by 0.4–4.8%. It stops billing at the same
     moment a park would and only drops the restore's billed WAKING
     (mean 0.90 s) and a park's SNAPSHOTTING (mean 0.25 s);
   - meterd's `usage_minutes` matched `instance_billing_intervals` to the
     second in all eight windows;
   - fleet, last 24 h: 81.1 GB-h billed, of which about 60 GB-h (≈74%) is
     apps waiting out their idle timeout (806 idle-outs, mean ≈554 s). Apps
     default to `when_idle`, so neither freeze nor unbilled quiet pause
     touches that.
2. **What a frozen VM holds.** All of its touched memory: a frozen VM keeps
   18–144 MB on small apps and 620–700 MB on a 600 MB heap. Proactive
   compression (ADR-20261031143521) only starts above 85% of a node's
   ceiling and compresses the whole VM; it ran 0 times in the last 24 h.
3. **Full compression** (zswap, lz4, memory-only, on fsn-2 and fsn-3):
   - about 3.9× (a 600 MB heap: 620 → 167 MB);
   - compressing costs ~1.5–2 ms of one host core per MB, off the request
     path;
   - a light request after the thaw costs +20–75 ms;
   - every compressed MB the guest touches later costs ~5.5–6 ms once
     (~21–25 µs per 4 KB page; item 5 splits this into decompression and
     second-level faults): +170 ms for a request that touches a 30 MB
     working set, 3.4–3.5 s to read 600 MB.

   Full compression can therefore wake slower than a snapshot restore.
4. **Cold-page compression.** On a 630 MB guest (600 MB written once,
   30 MB working set touched by every request): an MGLRU aging pass on the
   VM's memcg, then `memory.reclaim` coldest first.

   | Compressed | Frozen VM | First working-set request |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0% | 659–698 MB | 30–32 ms |
   | 75% | 301–302 MB | 32–33 ms |
   | 90% | 177–183 MB | 200–206 ms |

   At 90% the reclaim reached the working set. An aging pass costs about
   0.9 ms per MB touched next.
5. **Bulk prefetch.** The same 630 MB guest, fully compressed while frozen
   (670 → 181–187 MB). Before the thaw, the swapped pages were read back from
   the host through `/proc/PID/pagemap` and `/proc/PID/mem`, with 1 or 4
   threads:

   | Before the thaw | Read back 658 MB | First working-set request (30 MB) | Reading all 630 MB |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | not compressed (control) | — | 1.7–1.9 ms | 27–29 ms |
   | compressed, no prefetch | — | 168–181 ms | 3.36–3.50 s |
   | prefetch, 1 thread | 525–607 ms (1.1–1.3 GB/s) | 132–138 ms | 2.65–2.85 s |
   | prefetch, 4 threads | 227–332 ms (2.0–2.9 GB/s) | 132–140 ms | 2.69–2.76 s |

   - Bulk decompression on the host costs 0.35–0.9 ms per MB, against
     ~5.5 ms per MB (≈21 µs per page) when the guest faults pages back one
     at a time.
   - Prefetch removes only ~25% of what the guest pays. The other ~4.3 ms per
     MB (≈17 µs per page) is the guest's second-level (EPT) faults. Reclaim
     dropped those mappings, and every first touch still faults even when the
     page is back in host RAM.
   - On nested GCE, each of those faults exits to the outer hypervisor.
     Decompression is therefore not the dominant cost on these hosts.
6. **Caveat.** production-us compute nodes are GCE VMs, so tenant VMs run on
   nested KVM. Per-fault costs (items 3–5) are likely lower on the
   bare-metal reference node, and the constants below must be re-measured
   there before they are tuned.

### Constraints from the owner

- No level may add more than 100 ms to the first request.
- No level may wake slower than the level below it: a freeze must never wake
  slower than a snapshot restore.
- No control-plane Postgres on the request path.
- Billing stays plan RAM + 8 MB per billed second (§4.7).

## Decision

### 1. The ladder

Timers count from the end of the last request. Any request at any level
returns the instance to running.

| Level | Default entry | Where | Wake | RAM | Billed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 running | — | — | 0 | all | yes |
| L1 quiet pause | 1 s quiet | vmmd | ~1 ms | all | no |
| L2 freeze | 10 s quiet | vmmd | ~1 ms | all; admission books measured | no |
| L3 freeze, cold-compressed | memory untouched ≥ 1 min is compressed | vmmd | resume + faults on cold pages only | shrinks | no |
| L4 freeze, deeper | memory untouched ≥ 3 min is compressed, within the app's wake budget | vmmd | same | shrinks further | no |
| L5 snapshot | 10 min quiet, or earlier under node memory pressure, largest and longest idle first | schedd park | snapshot restore | 0 | no |

- These timers are defaults; §4 makes them per app.
- `api.QuietPauseAfterMillis` goes from 2000 to 1000.
- New limits (`IdleFreezeAfter`, `IdleColdAge1`, `IdleColdAge2`,
  `IdleSnapshotAfter`, `IdleWakeBudget`) go in `pkg/api/limits.go`.

### 2. A wake never costs more than the level below

Each level's wake budget is `min(100 ms, the app's measured restore time)` of
added first-request time.

- **How vmmd measures:** it times every wake from L3/L4 as the resume plus the
  fault time spent on compressed pages before the guest goes idle again
  (`vmmd_idle_wake_seconds{level}`).
- **Compression ceiling:** each app has one, as a page age. When a wake
  exceeds the budget, the ceiling moves to older pages only; when wakes stay
  under half the budget, it may move younger.
- **Full compression:** never used on a VM that may still wake. It stays a
  pressure step that runs immediately before a park.
- **Percentages:** compression is by age, never by percentage (90% reached
  the working set in the measurement).

### 3. L1–L4 live in vmmd; schedd keeps the instance state machine

L1–L4 are quiet pause extended: vmmd pauses the VM in place and then compresses
its cold pages by age.

- **Instance row:** stays RUNNING. There is no schedd thaw and no
  control-plane write per request, so the 30–85 ms publish step leaves these
  wakes.
- **State machine:** schedd remains the only writer to `instances` and owns
  the move to PARKED (L5).
- **Telemetry:** vmmd reports each instance's level, resident bytes and
  compressed bytes in `InstanceStats` / `InstanceTelemetry` (additive,
  `omitempty`).
- **Admission:** schedd books an instance at L2+ as frozen instances are
  booked today (`FrozenChargeMB`, measured with an 8 MB floor), leaves it out
  of the plan-reservation sum, and does not count its vCPU. The vCPU part
  already holds: L2+ instances are quiet-paused, and quiet pause admission
  discounts them. The floor ships with L1/L2 (#6739). Leaving L2+ out of the
  reservation sum needs the durable per-node guard
  (`pkg/state/node_reservation.go`) to take a RAM discount, the way it takes
  the quiet pause CPU discount. The guard sums RUNNING rows, and it must
  agree with the ledger, or wakes routed to a node the ledger sees room on
  are refused there.
- **Schedd FROZEN state:** stays only while existing freezes drain. New idle
  periods use the vmmd levels when the `idle_ladder` switch is on.

### 4. Per-app timing

- **Cron:** schedd files an ADR-160 prewarm intent for an app parked at L5
  before its next cron run, so the run lands on L0 instead of waiting on a
  restore. The prewarm drain already enforces plan limits, placement and
  RAM.
- **Adaptive timers (second step):** schedd keeps each app's recent
  inter-arrival times (`request_telemetry`). An app whose next request
  usually comes within the L5 timer stays at L3/L4 while the node has room;
  an app that goes quiet for hours goes down the ladder sooner.
- **Bounds:** the defaults in §1 stay the upper bound, and node pressure
  always wins.

### 5. Prefetch on wake

On a wake from L3 or L4, vmmd starts bringing the VM's compressed pages back
in the background, most recently used first. It runs alongside the resumed
guest, so pages are back before the guest asks for them.

On the nested production hosts this saves only ~25% (Context, item 5),
because the guest's second-level faults remain. So:

- **Budget:** the §2 wake budget is computed without prefetch.
- **Prefetch as an option:** prefetch ships behind its own switch, and only
  if the bare-metal measurement (Rollout step 1) shows the faults left after
  a prefetch are cheap there.
- **The larger win:** pre-populating the guest's second-level page tables
  after the prefetch would remove the remaining cost. `KVM_PRE_FAULT_MEMORY`
  (Linux 6.10+) does this, but it is a vCPU ioctl that Firecracker owns, so
  it needs a Firecracker change. It is measured before anyone builds it.

### 6. Pausing is safe by default

vmmd pauses a request-mode instance only when it has no request or stream in
flight and its cgroup CPU and tap traffic show it is idle.

- **Apps with background work:** an app whose timers, heartbeats or
  background threads keep it busy stays at L0 and stays billed, because it is
  working. This guard is what makes pausing a safe default for apps, not only
  for functions and `between_requests`.
- **Opt-out:** `lifecycle.pause: never` remains.
- **Never paused:** workers, services, jobs, app tasks, mirrors and
  private-network apps, as today.
- **Defaults:** new request-mode apps default to the guarded pause. Existing
  apps keep `when_idle` until they opt in (a customer-contract change, owner
  decision).

### 7. Billing

A billed second is an L0 second: plan RAM + 8 MB while the instance is
running and not paused.

- **L1–L4:** unbilled through ADR-20261023171905's ledger (vmmd reports,
  schedd writes `instance_quiet_pauses`, meterd subtracts what the ledger
  proves). Its failure rule stays: anything unproven is billed.
- **L5 and cold boot:** unchanged.
- **Capacity floors:** an app with `--min` still pays for its floor
  (ADR-060).

## Consequences

- **Customers:** billed for active time plus ~1 s per quiet period. Measured
  −79% to −88% for traffic with gaps, and the same for apps once the guarded
  pause is their default.
- **First request:**
  - L1 and L2: ~1 ms;
  - L3 and L4: within the 100 ms budget. Measured +2 ms on a 30 MB working
    set at 75% compression, plus ~25 ms for the aging pass on nested hosts;
  - L5: a restore, as today.
- **RAM:** a frozen VM shrinks with time. A 600 MB heap goes from ~680 MB to
  ~300 MB by L4, a small app from ~26 MB to ~10 MB, and L5 holds nothing.
- **Control plane:** fewer state changes than today's freeze and thaw, since
  L2–L4 are local. Pause ledger writes are batched per node every 2 s.
- **CPU:**
  - compression costs ~2 ms of a host core per MB, only for VMs quiet for
    at least a minute;
  - decompression on touch is charged to the VM's own vCPU time;
  - aging passes are per memcg and cheap to run, but add ~0.9 ms per MB on
    the next touch on nested hosts.
- **Invariant 2 (§6.2):** keeps its 47,600 MB ceiling. Under measured
  admission an L2+ instance counts at its measured charge, as frozen
  instances already do; the spec text must say so.
- **zswap on every compute node:** fsn-3 is a canary; fsn-2 was set up by
  hand on 2026-11-07 (PR #6721 lists it). A node without zswap runs L3/L4 as
  L2.
- **A vmmd restart:** compressed pages stay in zswap and fault back in on
  touch. The level resets to L2 for the new vmmd.

## Alternatives considered

- **Freeze in schedd at 10 s.** Every request after a 10 s gap would become
  a 60–130 ms thaw with control-plane writes, instead of a ~1 ms resume.
- **Full compression at freeze.** Reading a 600 MB heap back costs 3.4 s,
  slower than a restore, which breaks the monotonic rule.
- **Compression by a fixed percentage.** 90% reached the working set
  (+170 ms).
- **Early freeze (ADR-20261021215353).** It puts the control plane on every
  request; superseded by §3.
- **A fixed 10 min to snapshot for every app.** Kept as the default and upper
  bound; §4 shortens or extends it per app within node pressure.

## Rollout

1. Re-measure items 3–5 of Context on the bare-metal reference node and set
   the constants from that run.
2. Billing: pause ledger on, meterd `shadow` for a week, then `on`
   (ADR-20261023171905's steps).
3. vmmd `idle_ladder` switch with values off, observe and on. Observe
   records the level each VM would reach and the wake budget it would have
   used.
4. Cold-page compression with aging and the per-app ceiling. Prefetch only
   after step 1 shows it pays (§5).
5. Admission booking for L2+ instances (§3).
6. The activity guard and the new-app default (§6).
7. Cron pre-wake, then adaptive timers (§4).

## Open questions

- The L3/L4 page ages and how quickly the ceiling adapts.
- Runtimes whose garbage collector walks the whole heap soon after a wake
  (V8 major GC, the JVM). The per-app ceiling guards this, but it needs a
  measurement on real apps.
- Whether L5 should capture a warm snapshot (ADR-074) at idle-out when the
  app's state changed.
