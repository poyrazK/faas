# ADR-20261116163508 · Customer-granted support access

- **Status:** accepted (implemented dark behind the `support_grants` platform flag)
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Amends:** ADR-061 (organization authorization), ADR 20261023214253 (operator reads of customer data), ADR-20261114170352 (organization ownership guards)
- **Context:**
  - A customer who cannot deploy (broken CLI, CI credentials, a deploy they cannot diagnose) often asks support to "just deploy it". Operators cannot. The `/v1/admin/ops` surface only retries or cancels a deployment the customer already submitted, restarts or parks instances, and reads telemetry with `?on_behalf_of=`. The customer routes (`/v1/apps/{slug}/...`) authorize through `appaccess.authorize`, which admits only the app's personal owner or an organization member. Being an operator does not change that.
  - The only workaround today is that the customer invites a support person as an organization member. That gives full developer or admin rights with no end date, reaches every app, and the activity log does not show that the actor was support.
  - Operator-initiated access ("log in as the customer") would make one compromised operator session enough to change every customer's production. It would also undo the operator-session hardening of ADR 20261023214253 and contradict the zero customer-secret exposure posture (secretd, ADR-20261110210000).
- **Decision:**
  1. **Only the customer grants access.** An owner or admin of a shared organization, or the owner of a personal account, creates a *support grant* from a signed-in browser session, on the dashboard's Support access page or `POST /v1/orgs/{slug}/support-access/grants`, under the same step-up rule as ownership transfer (`requireStepUp`, 5 min, for accounts with two-factor authentication). API keys get 403 `browser_session_required`. An operator account can never grant or approve, including for an organization it belongs to. Every member can see the organization's grants and requests (`GET /v1/orgs/{slug}/support-access`); only owners and admins (`org.manage_support_access`) change them.
  2. **Support may ask, the customer decides.** An operator can file a *grant request* for an organization or personal account with a reason (`gregalectl support-access request --org <slug> --reason ...`, an operator mutation: MFA session and fresh step-up). The request mails the owners and admins (personal account: the owner) to approve or decline on the dashboard. A request expires after 24 h, sets nothing, and an organization has at most 3 open requests. Approving is the same browser-session action as granting, and the grant records the request.
  3. **Scope: deploy, rollback and restart only.** The grant names the whole organization or a list of its apps. Under a grant the operator may call only this closed route list (`appaccess.supportRoutes`), not everything `org.deploy_app` covers:
     - `POST /v1/apps/{slug}/deployments/source-ref`: builds the app's connected GitHub repository at its production branch, and nothing else. A commit SHA or another ref is refused: GitHub serves a fork's pull-request commits through the base repository, so either could name code support wrote itself, while only people with write access to the customer's repository can move its production branch.
     - `POST /v1/deployments/{id}/retry`, `POST /v1/apps/{slug}/deployments/{id}/cancel`.
     - `POST /v1/apps/{slug}/rollback`, `POST /v1/apps/{slug}/restart`.
     - The reads those steps need: the app, its deployments, the latest deployment, a deployment's summary, stages, timeline and build logs, and a build.

     Redeploying an earlier build is a retry or a rollback to a target deployment, so `POST /v1/apps/{slug}/deployments` (which takes an arbitrary image) is not on the list.

     Never under a grant: uploaded source (`source-tarball`, `/v1/uploads`, `dev-source`), secret or environment values or bindings, configuration, domains, edge rules, members, API or deploy tokens, billing, deletion, runtime logs, request data, traces, databases, storage and queues. Uploaded source is excluded because support-supplied code running with the app's secrets is exactly the exfiltration path this feature must not open. Request bodies are an allowlist (`supportBodyFields`): a source deploy may send `repo`, `ref` and `reason`; a rollback `target_deployment_id`, `expected_current_deployment_id` and `reason`; a retry `from_stage`; cancel and restart nothing. Every other field is refused, so support can never turn off a customer control (health checks, rollback on 5xx, canaries, freezes, triggers), pick another environment, notify other addresses, restore databases on rollback or forge an alert attribution, and a field added to these requests later stays refused until reviewed.
  4. **Expiry:** 24 h by default and 7 days at most, chosen at grant time. An owner or admin can revoke a grant at any moment, from the dashboard or `POST /v1/orgs/{slug}/support-access/grants/{id}/revoke` (a key may revoke: taking access away is always safe). A grant ends on its own when it expires, when its creator stops being an active owner or admin (for a personal organization, its owner), and when the organization is deleted; a suspended organization admits reads only.
  5. **Support acts as itself, never as the customer.** The operator calls the normal customer routes from its own session (`gregalectl auth login`) and names the grant on every request with `Support-Grant: <id>` (`gregalectl support-access deploy|retry|cancel|rollback|restart|status --grant <id> ...`). There is no ambient access: without the header an operator is an ordinary account. The request needs all of:
     - a session principal, not a key;
     - the operator allowlist and an enrolled authenticator, and for a write a step-up within 5 minutes, as for every provider mutation;
     - a usable grant that covers the app, with the `support_grants` flag on for its organization;
     - a route in `supportRoutes`.

     apid's `supportAccessGate`, just outside `appaccess.Wrap`, checks the session, operator, step-up, grant, flag and request body, then marks the request; `appaccess.authorize` checks the support path first, before the personal/shared split, so it works for personal accounts too, and checks the app and route. The resulting `Grant` has `Actor` = operator, `Owner` = the app's resource account (so billing and quotas are the customer's as usual) and a fence with `SupportGrantID`. Handlers that re-check the organization role see `developer`, the least role that deploys; the route list, not the role, bounds support. The operator's own memberships play no part.
  6. **Revocation stops work in flight.** `OrgAccessFence` gains `SupportGrantID`. `lockOrgAccess` takes a share lock on the grant row and refuses a write unless the grant is still usable (unrevoked, unexpired, creator still an active owner or admin), so a revoke cancels a deploy admission that has not yet committed, as member removal already does.
  7. **The customer sees everything.** Every request under a grant writes a `support.action` audit event (operator, grant, organization, app, route, status, deployment id), and every change also writes a `support_access.action` row to the organization's activity log, labelled "Gregale support (<operator>)", next to the activity the deploy, rollback or restart itself records. Grants, approvals, declines, revocations and requests are activity rows too. Owners and admins (a personal organization's owner) are mailed when support requests access, when access is granted or approved (with its end time), when it is revoked, and when support changes something, at most once per `SupportActionMailInterval` (10 minutes) per recipient. There is no separate expiry mail; the grant mail states the end time.
  8. **Rollout.** Behind the platform flag `support_grants`, decided per organization and off everywhere, whose kill switch refuses every grant at once. Before it is enabled on prod, the Terms of Service and privacy notice describe customer-granted support access.
- **Data model:** `support_grants(id, org_id, app_ids uuid[] NULL = whole org, created_by_account_id, request_id NULL, reason, created_at, expires_at, revoked_at, revoked_by_account_id)` and `support_grant_requests(id, org_id, operator_account_id, reason, status open|approved|declined, created_at, expires_at, decided_at, decided_by_account_id)`. CHECKs cap a grant at 7 days and 100 apps and a request at 24 hours; the limits live in `pkg/api/limits.go`. Personal accounts are addressed through their personal organization row, so one table serves both.
- **API surface:** while dark, the routes stay out of the public OpenAPI spec and SDKs (listed in both `routeExclude` lists), like other flag-gated previews.
- **Consequences:**
  - A customer who can't deploy gets help within minutes, without handing out a permanent seat. The "invite support as a member" workaround can be retired from the runbooks.
  - A stolen operator session reaches only the customers that granted access right now, and only deploy, rollback and restart. It cannot read secrets or upload code.
  - Support cannot fix a customer whose problem needs a code or configuration change. That stays the customer's work, deliberately.
  - Two more tables and a fence column. Every new customer route stays outside the support path until it is added to `supportRoutes`; a test keeps every entry a reviewed app route whose action is a read or a deploy.
- **Slices:**
  1. Done: schema, stores, flag, grants and requests (API and dashboard), the gate and `appaccess` support path, the fence, audit, activity and mail, and `gregalectl support-access`.
  2. Customer CLI (`gregale support-access list|revoke`), the runbook, and the Terms of Service / privacy text, before the flag is turned on for anyone.
  3. Project routes (`/v1/projects/{slug}/deploy`, release-set rollback), as a separate reviewed list.
