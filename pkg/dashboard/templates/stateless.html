What this is

onebox faas is stateless backend compute: each invocation boots a fresh microVM, runs your handler, and the VM is torn down on park. There is no on-disk state between invocations. State lives in managed services you point your handler at — Neon for Postgres, Upstash for Redis, S3 for blobs, etc. — and the connection details travel as sealed secrets.

The platform enforces this in two layers: accept-time rejects stateful base images (postgres, redis, mysql, etc.) with a 422 pointing at a managed alternative; runtime watches a small closed path set inside the guest and writes a stateless.advisory audit row when your handler writes to one of them. Advisories are observation, not enforcement — your wake still completes — but they show up here so you can see what your code is trying to do.
